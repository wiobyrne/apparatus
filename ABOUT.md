# About Apparatus

## A public practice of digital literacy

Apparatus is a way of designing, writing, and documenting digital spaces so
they help people read, orient, and exercise judgment.

It begins with a simple idea: when people land on a page, they should be able
to tell what it is, what matters there, and where to go next without having to
fight the interface. But it is more than a set of visual rules. It is a public
practice of asking how the tools and spaces we use shape attention,
authorship, and choice.

## What it holds

Apparatus has four related parts:

- a design grammar for making readable digital spaces
- a public record of decisions, revisions, constraints, and examples
- a small practice of publishing autonomy: setting more of the terms around
  where work lives and how it is read
- teaching material that others can adapt, question, or reject

The sites are live experiments. Apparatus is the public notebook. Essays and
posts translate individual moments of practice into teaching.

## Print to pixel

The recurring question is not how to make the web look like print. It is what
print taught us about attention, sequence, and reading, what digital spaces
can add, and how to shape both into humane environments for reading and
writing.

That means asking questions in real work:

- What does a page invite a reader to do?
- What do links, revision, connection, and return make possible?
- What do particular tools invite or distort?
- How can AI assist drafting, research, pattern-finding, or iteration without
  quietly displacing authorship and judgment?

## Where it can be used

Apparatus can move across publishing spaces:

- websites
- notes and digital gardens
- newsletters
- slide decks
- diagrams and teaching visuals

The expression changes by medium; the reading contract remains recognizable.

## What it is not

Apparatus is not a theme, a component library, or a universal answer to the
conditions set by platforms and institutions. Owning and shaping a publishing
space does not substitute for collective power. It can, however, make the
terms of digital work easier to inspect, discuss, and teach.

## In one sentence

Apparatus is a portable reading framework and a public practice of digital
literacy.
