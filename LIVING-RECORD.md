# A Living Record

Apparatus changes because it is used. A new page, a new publishing surface, or
a reader's need can reveal that an old rule no longer helps. This record makes
those changes visible without pretending that the earlier work never existed.

## Why keep a record?

A website can make itself look finished even while the thinking behind it is
still changing. Apparatus keeps a visible history so readers can see that a
design is not simply applied once. It is built, tested, reconsidered, and
revised.

That history matters because the work is about more than appearance. It shows
how decisions about pages, publishing, and tools develop through use.

## What a version means

A version is a named checkpoint. It says: this is what Apparatus means now,
this is what changed, and this is why the change matters.

A version is not a score, a product launch, or a claim that the work is
finished. The current `v0.x` series means that Apparatus is still being tested
and refined in public. A future `v1.0` would mean that its core agreement is
stable enough to rely on, not that the inquiry has ended.

Not every edit needs a version. A clearer sentence or a repaired link can be
updated directly. A version is for a coherent moment when several related
choices have settled into a useful agreement.

## How to read the record

There are three ways to follow a change:

| If you want to know… | Read… |
| --- | --- |
| What Apparatus means now | the current documents, beginning with [`APPARATUS.md`](APPARATUS.md) |
| Why an important choice changed | the dated notes in [`decisions/`](decisions/) |
| What changed between checkpoints | [`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md) |

The history records changes to the work and the agreements behind it. It is not
a ledger of every conversation, draft, or tool involved in making a change.

## What counts as a meaningful change

A meaningful change is one that affects how a reader encounters, understands,
or moves through the work. It might be a new shared rule, a better way to adapt
the system to another surface, or a lesson learned from something that did not
work.

The v0.5 update is one example. It clarified the type roles, made the finder
the shared identity mark, and gave share cards a single readable structure.
Those were not only visual adjustments; they made the same reading posture
more dependable across a site, a social preview, a slide, and a newsletter.

## Images, tables, and figures are part of the reading environment

An Open Graph image is often the first part of a page someone sees. It may
appear in a message, a social post, or a search result before the page itself
is opened. Apparatus treats that image as a small reading surface: it should
make the title, description, and source clear enough to orient someone rather
than simply advertise at them.

Tables and figures do related work inside a page. A table should make a real
comparison easier to see; a figure should make a relationship, sequence, or
idea easier to understand. Neither exists only to decorate an essay. When a
new pattern for these elements proves useful across the work, it can become an
Apparatus decision and eventually a versioned change.

## What comes next

Over time, this record can also look backward. Archived versions of the sites
can show how the spaces changed before Apparatus had a name: what each period
was trying to do, what it enabled, and what it could not yet see. That longer
history is a lineage of the practice, not a claim that every change was a
straight line toward the present.
