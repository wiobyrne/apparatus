# Decision: Apparatus as Literacy Work in Public

**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Status:** Accepted

## Context

Apparatus began as documentation for a visual system and a way of bringing a
shared reading posture across publishing surfaces. That remains necessary, but
it does not fully describe the work.

The system emerged from a longer practice of teaching people how to read,
write, and make sense of digital spaces. Its design decisions also raise
questions about the affordances of tools and platforms: what they make easier,
what they obscure, and where judgment and authorship remain located. AI is one
such tool; it belongs in the inquiry without becoming the project’s sole
subject.

## Decision

Apparatus is the durable container for a public practice of digital literacy.
It remains a portable reading framework for digital publishing, while also
recording how that framework is designed, used, tested, and revised.

The public framing is:

> Apparatus is a public practice of digital literacy: an evolving record of
> how I design, write, and work with digital tools to make spaces that help
> people read, orient, and exercise judgment.

*Print to Pixel* is an essay series and public doorway into this work, not a
replacement name for Apparatus.

## Implications

- The public documents continue to hold shared design rules, surface guidance,
  decision records, and examples.
- Meaningful changes to the public contract should explain both what changed
  and why it changed.
- Apparatus records public commitments and consequential decisions, not a
  complete private journal or a tool-by-tool provenance trail.
- AI may be discussed where it materially changes method, judgment,
  authorship, or a design decision. It does not require routine labels,
  authorship badges, or boilerplate disclosure.
- Apparatus offers a small practice of publishing autonomy; it does not claim
  that individual control over a site substitutes for collective power.

## What does not change

This decision does not turn Apparatus into an AI framework, a generic workflow
system, or a component library. Its first responsibility remains creating
readable, navigable, humane publishing spaces.

The existing shared design rules and surface adaptations remain in force.
